I am an observational stellar astrophysicist and [NSERC PGS-D](https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/en/funding-opportunity/archived-nserc-postgraduate-scholarships-doctoral-program) supported PhD candidate in the Department of Physics, Engineering Physics & Astronomy at [Queen's University](https://www.queensu.ca/physics/people-search/james-barron), Canada. My PhD supervisor is [Dr. Gregg Wade](https://www.rmc-cmr.ca/en/physics-space-science/gregg-wade). 

## Research
My research seeks to answer fundamental questions about the origin, impact, and evolution of stellar magnetic fields. In particular, I am interested in the complex relationship between magnetic fields and stellar processes, including pulsation, rotation and binary interactions. My work spans the upper Hertzsprung-Russell diagram, from massive main-sequence [OB-type stars](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/adf6d9) through later evolutionary stages, including [classical Cepheids](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stac565).

The principal tool of my research is [spectropolarimetry](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.00179), which enables the detection and characterization of stellar magnetic fields to [sub-Gauss precision](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stac565). I interpret these magnetic fields in the context of individual stellar systems and broader stellar populations through complementary analysis of time-series photometry and high-resolution spectroscopy. My work utilizes diverse astronomical observations, from ground-based facilities including the [Canada-France-Hawaii Telescope](https://www.cfht.hawaii.edu/), the [ESO 3.6-m Telescope](https://www.eso.org/public/teles-instr/lasilla/36/), and the [Gemini Observatory](https://www.gemini.edu/), as well as space missions such as [TESS](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/tess/), [Kepler](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/kepler/), and [Gaia](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Gaia).
