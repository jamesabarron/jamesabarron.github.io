I am an observational stellar astrophysicist and NSERC PGS-D supported PhD candidate in the Department of Physics, Engineering Physics & Astronomy at [Queen's University](https://www.queensu.ca/physics/people-search/james-barron), Canada. My PhD supervisor is [Dr. Gregg Wade](https://www.rmc-cmr.ca/en/physics-space-science/gregg-wade). 

### Research
My research seeks to answer fundamental questions about the origin, impact, and evolution of stellar magnetic fields. In particular, I am interested in understanding the complex relationship between magnetic fields and stellar processes, including pulsation, rotation, and binary interactions. My research involves the analysis of diverse astronomical observations, including ground-based data obtained with the [Canada-France-Hawaii Telescope](https://www.cfht.hawaii.edu/), the [ESO 3.6-m telescope](https://www.eso.org/public/teles-instr/lasilla/36/), and the [Gemini Observatory](https://www.gemini.edu/), as well as space-based observations from missions such as [TESS](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/tess/), [Kepler](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/kepler/), and [Gaia](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Gaia).
